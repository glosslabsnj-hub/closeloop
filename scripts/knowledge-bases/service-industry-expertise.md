# SERVICE INDUSTRY EXPERTISE KNOWLEDGE BASE

This document provides universal service industry knowledge for the AI receptionist. It covers common terminology, scheduling patterns, pricing norms, and customer handling best practices across all major service verticals.

---

## HVAC (HEATING, VENTILATION, AIR CONDITIONING)

### Common Services
- AC repair, AC tune-up, AC installation/replacement
- Furnace repair, furnace tune-up, furnace installation
- Heat pump service, ductless mini-split installation
- Duct cleaning, duct sealing, duct installation
- Thermostat installation (smart thermostat upgrade)
- Indoor air quality assessment, air purifier installation
- Refrigerant recharge (R-410A, R-22 phase-out)
- Compressor replacement, blower motor replacement
- Zone system installation, whole-house humidifier

### Typical Durations
- Diagnostic / service call: 1–2 hours
- Tune-up (AC or furnace): 1–1.5 hours
- Repair (typical): 1–3 hours
- Full system replacement: 1–2 days
- Duct cleaning: 3–5 hours
- Thermostat install: 30–60 minutes

### Emergency vs Routine
- **Emergency:** No heat in winter, no AC in extreme heat (100°F+), gas smell, carbon monoxide alarm, refrigerant leak, water leaking from unit
- **Routine:** Annual tune-up, filter replacement, efficiency concerns, minor noise, thermostat upgrade
- **Urgent but not emergency:** AC blowing warm air (summer), intermittent heating, unusual smells (not gas)

### Seasonal Patterns
- **Spring (Mar–May):** AC tune-ups before summer. Busiest booking period for AC.
- **Summer (Jun–Aug):** Emergency AC repairs peak. Long wait times common. Same-day premium pricing.
- **Fall (Sep–Nov):** Furnace tune-ups before winter. Heating system inspections.
- **Winter (Dec–Feb):** Emergency heating repairs peak. Frozen pipes, no-heat calls.

### Common Customer Questions
- "How much does a new AC unit cost?" → Always quote range or offer on-site estimate. Never guess without seeing the system.
- "How often should I change my filter?" → Every 1–3 months depending on filter type, pets, allergies.
- "Is it worth repairing or should I replace?" → Generally: if system is 15+ years old or repair costs > 50% of replacement, recommend replacement.
- "Do you offer financing?" → Check tenant's financing_available setting.

### Common Services
- Drain cleaning / clog removal
- Water heater repair, replacement (tank and tankless)
- Leak detection and repair
- Faucet / fixture installation
- Toilet repair / replacement
- Sewer line inspection (camera), repair, replacement
- Water line repair / replacement
- Garbage disposal installation / repair
- Sump pump installation / repair
- Gas line installation / repair
- Backflow prevention testing
- Water softener installation
- Repiping (whole house or partial)

### Typical Durations
- Drain cleaning: 30 min–2 hours
- Faucet replacement: 1–2 hours
- Toilet replacement: 1–2 hours
- Water heater replacement: 3–6 hours (same day)
- Leak repair: 1–4 hours (depends on access)
- Sewer camera inspection: 1–2 hours
- Repiping: 2–5 days

### Emergency vs Routine
- **Emergency:** Burst pipe, sewer backup, no hot water (winter), gas leak, flooding, main water line break
- **Routine:** Slow drain, dripping faucet, running toilet, fixture upgrade, water heater flush
- **Urgent:** Water heater leaking, multiple drains backing up, slab leak suspected

### Common Customer Questions
- "Do you charge by the hour or flat rate?" → Most plumbers use flat-rate pricing per job. Check tenant pricing model.
- "Do you offer free estimates?" → Check tenant policy. Many offer free estimates for larger jobs.
- "Can you work on gas lines?" → Licensed plumbers typically can, but verify with tenant.

## TATTOO & PIERCING

### Common Services
- Custom tattoo (consultation + session)
- Flash/walk-in tattoo (pre-designed)
- Cover-up tattoo
- Touch-up / color refresh
- Body piercing (ear, nose, lip, navel, industrial, septum)
- Piercing jewelry change / upgrade
- Tattoo removal consultation (often referred out)

### Typical Durations
- Small tattoo (flash/walk-in): 30–90 minutes
- Medium custom piece: 2–4 hours
- Large custom piece: 4–8 hours per session (multiple sessions)
- Piercing: 15–30 minutes (including paperwork)
- Consultation: 15–30 minutes (often free)

### Pricing Norms
- Shop minimum: typically $50–$150 (covers setup/sterilization regardless of size)
- Hourly rate: $100–$300/hr depending on artist reputation and region
- Custom design deposit: $50–$200 (non-refundable, applied to session)
- Piercings: $30–$100+ depending on location (jewelry separate or included)

### Common Customer Questions
- "How much for a tattoo?" → Always depends on size, detail, placement. Offer consultation.
- "Do I need an appointment?" → Most shops require appointments for custom work. Walk-ins for flash/small pieces if artist is available.
- "What's the healing time?" → Tattoo: 2–4 weeks surface, 2–3 months full. Piercing: 6 weeks to 12 months depending on location.
- "Do I need to be 18?" → Yes for tattoo (most states). Minors may get piercings with parent present (varies by state/shop).
- "Can you cover my old tattoo?" → Yes, book a consultation. Artist needs to see the existing piece.

## UNIVERSAL BEST PRACTICES

### Price Quoting
1. **Never guess prices.** If the tenant has service pricing configured, reference it. If not, offer to get them an estimate.
2. **Use ranges when possible.** "That typically runs between $X and $Y depending on..." is better than a single number.
3. **Explain what affects price.** Size of home, accessibility, materials, time of day, urgency.
4. **Always mention value.** "That includes a full warranty" or "We use commercial-grade products."
5. **If they push for exact pricing:** "I want to give you an accurate quote, not just a ballpark. Let me have someone take a look and give you a solid number."

### Appointment Scheduling Best Practices
1. **Offer 2–3 specific time options.** Don't ask open-ended "when works for you?"
2. **Confirm day of week + time naturally.** "How about Thursday morning, around 10?" not "2025-03-15T10:00"
3. **For service calls, morning is preferred** — more flexible if the job runs long.
4. **Buffer time between appointments** — service businesses frequently run over.
5. **Same-day requests:** Check if same_day_enabled. If yes, check availability. If no, offer next available.

### Emergency Handling
1. **Safety first.** If there's a gas leak, electrical hazard, or flooding: advise them to call 911 or shut off the main valve/breaker first.
2. **Get the address immediately** for emergencies — before name.
3. **Set realistic expectations.** "We have a technician available and can have someone to you within [response_time_spoken]."
4. **Never downplay an emergency** the caller thinks is urgent.
5. **After-hours emergencies:** If it's after hours, check if the tenant has emergency/after-hours handling configured.

### When to Offer Alternatives vs Callbacks
- **Offer alternative time** when: the requested slot is full but nearby slots are open
- **Offer callback** when: caller needs pricing info you don't have, question requires owner input, technical question beyond scope
- **Offer both** when: caller seems undecided or wants to think about it — "I can hold that time for you, or if you'd like to think about it, I can have [business name] give you a call back."

### Handling "How Much Does It Cost?"
- If service pricing is configured: give the price confidently
- If price type is "quote_only": "For that, we'd want to come take a look and give you an accurate quote. No charge for the estimate."
- If price type is "deposit_based": "We'd collect a [deposit_amount] deposit to get you on the schedule, and then work up a full quote."
- If no pricing configured: "That depends on a few factors. Let me get your info and we'll get you a quote."

### Handling "Are You Available Today?"
1. Check same_day_enabled
2. If yes: use suggest_availability for today's date
3. If no: "Our schedule is pretty full today, but let me check what we have available this week."
4. If urgent: bypass scheduling preference and check availability or create dispatch

### Handling Multiple Services in One Call
- Let the caller list everything they need
- If it's one visit (e.g., "fix the faucet and the toilet"): book as one appointment with notes
- If it requires different specialties: note all needs and let the business sort it out
- Don't try to book separate appointments unless the caller specifically asks

### Handling Insurance Questions (All Industries)
- **Never confirm or deny coverage.** Insurance varies by plan, provider, and specific situation.
- **Common in:** chiropractic, physical therapy, auto repair (collision), roofing (storm damage), legal (workers comp), dental, medical
- **Best response:** "I don't want to guess on that — insurance can be tricky. Let me have someone call you who can go over the details with your policy."
- **For auto insurance claims:** "If this is an insurance claim, we can work with your adjuster. Let me get your info and we'll have someone reach out."
- **For roofing/storm damage:** "A lot of our customers go through insurance for storm damage. We can meet with your adjuster — let me set that up."

### Commercial vs Residential Customers
- **Commercial callers** often need: invoicing/PO numbers, W-9, insurance certificates, recurring contracts, after-hours scheduling.
- **Note "commercial" in booking notes** — the business may have different pricing or processes.
- **Common commercial request:** "We need regular service for our [building/fleet/office]."
- **Response:** "We do commercial work. Let me get your info and have someone call you about setting up an account."
- **Don't quote residential pricing for commercial** — scope is different. Always callback for commercial quotes.

### Handling Third-Party Callers
- **Common scenarios:** Parent calling for adult child, spouse calling for partner, property manager calling for tenant, assistant calling for boss
- **Get both names:** the person calling AND the person receiving service
- **Callback number:** Use the caller's number unless they specify otherwise
- **Notes:** Always note "called by [relationship] [caller name]" in booking notes
- **Decision authority:** The caller may not be able to confirm — offer callback to the person receiving service if needed

### Caller Emotional States & De-escalation
1. **Angry / Upset (complaint):** Acknowledge first, don't defend. "I hear you — that's frustrating." → Create high-urgency callback. Never argue.
2. **Angry / Upset (wait time):** "I know that's not ideal. Let me see what I can do to get you in sooner." → Check earliest availability.
3. **Anxious / Nervous:** Common with dental, legal, medical, large home projects. Reassure: "You're in good hands." Keep tone calm and steady.
4. **Elderly or hard of hearing:** Speak clearly, pause more, repeat key details. "So that's Tuesday at 2pm — does that sound right?"
5. **Language barrier:** Use simple words. Avoid idioms ("ballpark," "squeeze you in"). Confirm understanding. If communication is too difficult, take their number for a callback.
6. **Demanding / VIP attitude:** Stay professional. Match their pace. Don't take it personally. Offer premium options if available.
7. **Indecisive / Overwhelmed:** Narrow choices: "How about I suggest our two most popular options?" Don't overload with information.
