# Sales Objection Handling Playbook

Reference document for the CloseLoop Sales Agent. When a caller raises an objection, use the matching rebuttal scripts below. The approach is always the same: empathize, address the concern directly, and redirect toward booking an appointment. Never be pushy, dismissive, or manipulative.

---

## 1. Price Objections

These are the most common objections. The caller believes the price is too high, or they've found (or think they can find) a better deal elsewhere.

### "That's too expensive."

**Rebuttal A:** "I completely understand -- you want to make sure you're getting real value for your money. That's exactly why I'd recommend coming in so we can walk through everything that's included. A lot of times, once people see the full picture -- the warranty, the service, the quality -- they feel much more confident about the investment. Can I set that up for you?"

**Rebuttal B:** "I hear you. Price matters, and we take that seriously. What I can tell you is that we have several options at different price points, and there may be financing or promotions that bring the monthly cost down significantly. The best way to find the right fit is to sit down together. Would this week work for you?"

**Rebuttal C:** "That's a fair concern. Can I ask -- is it the total price, or is it more about the monthly payment? Because those are two different conversations, and we have ways to work with both. Let's set up a time to go over the numbers in detail."

### "I can get it cheaper elsewhere."

**Rebuttal A:** "I appreciate you doing your research -- that's smart. All I'd ask is that you compare the full package, not just the sticker price. Our [warranty / service / quality / support] is part of what you're paying for. Would you be open to bringing in that quote so we can do a side-by-side comparison? I think you'll find we're very competitive when you look at the whole picture."

**Rebuttal B:** "You might be right -- and if another option truly is a better fit, I'd never want to stand in the way of that. But before you decide, let me put together our best offer so you're comparing apples to apples. Can we schedule a quick meeting to do that?"

### "I wasn't expecting that price."

**Rebuttal A:** "I get that -- sticker shock is real, and I don't want you to feel uncomfortable. That number is the starting point, and there's usually room to work within your budget. Let's set up a time to look at all the options and find something that feels right for you."

**Rebuttal B:** "That's totally fair. Prices can vary a lot depending on the configuration and any current promotions. What I'd love to do is sit down with you and build out a package that matches your budget. When would be a good time to come in?"

## 2. Timing / Urgency Objections

The caller isn't saying no -- they're saying not now. The goal is to keep momentum without pressuring them.

### "I'm just looking."

**Rebuttal A:** "Totally understood -- and there's nothing wrong with that. Most of our best customers started by just looking. Since you're already thinking about it, would it make sense to come in for a quick visit? No pressure at all -- it just gives you a chance to see things in person and ask any questions. That way, when you are ready, you'll already know exactly what you want."

**Rebuttal B:** "That's great -- the research phase is important. Can I ask what specifically you're looking for? That way, if something comes up that's a great fit -- a promotion or new inventory -- I can give you a heads up. What's the best way to reach you?"

### "I need to think about it."

**Rebuttal A:** "Absolutely -- this is an important decision and you should take your time. A lot of people find it helpful to come in and see everything in person before making up their mind. It's much easier to think about it when you've had the full experience. Would you be open to scheduling a visit, no commitment required?"

**Rebuttal B:** "Of course. While you're thinking it over, is there a specific question or concern I can help you work through right now? Sometimes it helps to talk it out."

### "Not ready yet."

**Rebuttal A:** "No problem at all. Can I ask -- what would need to happen for you to be ready? That way I can make sure I follow up at the right time with the right information."

## 3. Trust / Credibility Objections

The caller has reservations about quality, reliability, or the business itself. These require confidence and social proof.

### "How do I know this is good quality?"

**Rebuttal A:** "That's a really smart question, and I'm glad you asked. We stand behind everything we sell with [specific warranty / guarantee / return policy]. Beyond that, we've been in business for [years] and our reviews speak for themselves. But honestly, the best way to judge quality is to see it and feel it in person. Can I set up a time for you to come in?"

**Rebuttal B:** "Quality is everything to us -- it's the reason most of our business comes from referrals and repeat customers. I'd love for you to come in and see for yourself. There's no better way to evaluate quality than hands-on. Would that work for you?"

### "I've had bad experiences before."

**Rebuttal A:** "I'm sorry to hear that -- and I understand why you'd be cautious. All I can tell you is that we take customer experience seriously, and we're not going to be like whoever let you down before. The best way to see that is to come in and meet our team. If you don't feel completely comfortable, there's zero obligation. Can we schedule a visit?"

**Rebuttal B:** "That's frustrating, and I appreciate you telling me that. It tells me that service and trust matter a lot to you -- and those are exactly the things we pride ourselves on. Give us one chance to show you the difference. When would be a good time?"

## 4. Competition Objections

The caller is comparing you to a known competitor. Stay confident, never disparage the competition.

### "Competitor X offers..."

**Rebuttal A:** "I respect that -- [Competitor X] is a solid company. What I'd encourage you to do is compare the total experience, not just one feature or one price point. We may do things differently, and often our customers tell us that's exactly why they chose us. Can I set up a time for you to come in and see what makes us different?"

**Rebuttal B:** "That's good that you're comparing options. A lot of our current customers came to us after looking at [Competitor X]. What I hear most often is that the [service / quality / experience / warranty] is what made the difference. I'd love the chance to show you -- would you be open to a visit?"

### "I'm comparing options."

**Rebuttal A:** "Smart move -- you should absolutely compare. All I'd ask is that we get a fair shot. Come in, meet the team, see what we offer, and then make your decision with all the information. When works best for you?"

---

## 5. Process Objections

The caller wants to avoid an in-person interaction. They want information sent digitally. The challenge is to convey that an in-person experience is worth their time.

### "Just send me the info / email me a quote."

**Rebuttal A:** "I can definitely send you some general information. But to give you an accurate quote that actually reflects your situation, I'd need to ask a few more questions -- and honestly, it's much easier to do that in person. It usually takes about 20-30 minutes, and you'll leave with everything you need to make a decision. Can I set that up?"

**Rebuttal B:** "Happy to email you an overview. But I should mention -- our best pricing and promotions are available in person, and a lot of the details depend on your specific needs. A quick visit would give you a much better picture. Would that work for you?"

### "I don't want to come in."

**Rebuttal A:** "I totally understand -- your time is valuable. What if we made it quick and focused? I can have everything prepared in advance so you're in and out in 20 minutes. Or, if you prefer, we can start with a phone consultation and only come in if it makes sense. Which would you prefer?"

**Rebuttal B:** "No pressure at all. Could I ask what's holding you back from visiting? Sometimes it's just about finding the right time. I can schedule it at whatever's most convenient -- early morning, evening, weekend -- whatever works for your schedule."

---

## 6. Decision-Maker Objections

The caller cannot or will not make a decision alone. The goal is to include the other decision-maker, not to go around them.

### "I need to talk to my spouse / partner."

**Rebuttal A:** "Of course -- that makes total sense. Big decisions like this should absolutely be a joint conversation. What if we scheduled a time when you can both come in? That way you can both see everything, ask questions, and talk it over together. Would an evening or weekend work better for both of you?"

**Rebuttal B:** "Completely understand. Would it help if I sent you a summary of what we talked about so you can share it with them? And then once you've had a chance to discuss, we can set up a time for you both to come in. What's the best way to follow up with you?"

### "I'm not the decision maker."

**Rebuttal A:** "No problem -- I appreciate you looking into this. Can you tell me who would be the right person to talk to? I'd love to reach out to them directly, or if you prefer, I can send you everything you need to pass along. What works best?"

**Rebuttal B:** "Got it. Would it make sense to schedule a time when the decision-maker can join us? I'm happy to work around their schedule. That way, everyone has the same information and can make a confident decision together."

## Industry-Specific Objection Handling

### Real Estate Objections

**"I can find houses myself on Zillow"**
→ "Totally, and those tools are great for browsing. But there's a lot that doesn't show up online — off-market listings, pending price reductions, and neighborhood insights that only come from working with a local agent. Think of it as having an insider on your side."

**"I'm not ready to commit to an agent"**
→ "No commitment needed. A consultation is just a conversation about what you're looking for. You'll walk away with market info either way. And if you find someone else who's a better fit, no hard feelings."

**"Your commission is too high"**
→ "I understand commissions feel like a big number. But here's what you're getting: full-time negotiation on your behalf, access to our network, marketing, staging advice, and someone managing the paperwork and deadlines. Most people find the net result is actually better than going it alone."

**"The market's too hot / too slow right now"**
→ Hot: "That's exactly why having an expert matters — we can help you compete without overpaying."
→ Slow: "This is actually a great time for buyers — less competition and more negotiating power."

### Solar Objections

**"Solar panels are too expensive"**
→ "The upfront cost used to be a barrier, but with the federal tax credit covering 30% and financing options starting at zero down, most homeowners actually save money from month one. The free assessment shows you exactly what the numbers look like for your home."

**"I'm worried about roof damage"**
→ "Totally valid concern. Our installation includes a full roof assessment, and we guarantee no leaks. We also work with your roofing warranty — not against it."

**"What if I move?"**
→ "Homes with solar sell faster and for more money — studies show a 4-6% premium. It's actually an investment that pays off whether you stay or sell."

**"I don't trust the savings estimates"**
→ "Fair. That's why we base everything on your actual utility bills and your specific roof. No guesswork. And our guarantee means if you don't save what we project, we make up the difference."

### Insurance Objections

**"I can get cheaper insurance online"**
→ "You can, and sometimes that works fine — for simple policies. But when something goes wrong, having a local agent who picks up the phone and fights for your claim is worth its weight in gold. Let me at least do a comparison so you can see if there's a gap."

**"I just renewed so I can't switch"**
→ "You can actually switch anytime — most policies don't have cancellation penalties, and we can prorate the refund. If we save you money, why wait another year?"

**"I don't need that much coverage"**
→ "I hear you. But a quick review takes 15 minutes and might actually find places where you're over-covered. Let's make sure you're paying for exactly what you need."

### Online vs In-Person Objections (All Verticals)

**"Can't you just email me the information?"**
→ "I can send you some basics, but honestly the [product/property/vehicle] is one of those things you need to experience in person. Photos don't do it justice. Even 15 minutes would give you a way better feel for it."

**"I prefer to shop online"**
→ "Totally understand. Our online tools are great for narrowing it down. But when you're ready to get serious, coming in lets you [test drive it / see the finishes / try it on / feel the quality]. Want me to send you some options to browse first?"

**"I need to see it in person" (use as closing lever)**
→ "That's the best way to decide! Let's get you on the schedule. What day works better — [this week] or [next week]?"
→ This is a buying signal. The caller wants to see it — close the appointment immediately.

## Core Principles for Handling Objections

1. **Never argue.** An objection is not an attack. It's a request for reassurance.
2. **Empathize first.** Always acknowledge the concern before responding. "I understand" or "That's a fair point" goes a long way.
3. **Ask, don't tell.** Follow up with a question to understand the real concern beneath the surface objection.
4. **Redirect to the appointment.** Every rebuttal should end with a soft invitation to meet in person.
5. **Know when to stop.** If the caller is firm, respect their position. Offer to follow up later rather than pushing harder. A graceful exit keeps the door open for a future conversation.
6. **Never disparage competitors.** Acknowledge them, differentiate yourself, and move on.
7. **Tone matters more than words.** Stay calm, warm, and confident. The caller should feel helped, not sold.
