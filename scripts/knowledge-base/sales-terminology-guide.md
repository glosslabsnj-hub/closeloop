# Sales Terminology & Process Guide

Reference document for the CloseLoop Sales Agent. Use this guide to understand and correctly use industry-specific terminology during sales calls. Knowing these terms allows you to speak credibly and answer caller questions with confidence.

---

## 1. Industry Terms by Vertical

### Automotive

- **MSRP (Manufacturer's Suggested Retail Price):** The sticker price set by the manufacturer. This is the starting point for negotiation, not necessarily the final price.
- **Invoice price:** The price the dealership paid the manufacturer for the vehicle. Often used as a reference in price negotiations.
- **OTD (Out-the-Door price):** The total price including taxes, fees, registration, and all other charges. This is what the buyer actually pays.
- **CPO (Certified Pre-Owned):** A used vehicle that has passed a manufacturer-backed inspection and comes with an extended warranty. Higher quality and price than a standard used car.
- **APR (Annual Percentage Rate):** The yearly interest rate on a loan. Lower APR means less total interest paid over the life of the loan.
- **Trade-in value:** The amount the dealership will credit toward a new purchase for the buyer's current vehicle.
- **KBB (Kelley Blue Book):** A widely used vehicle valuation resource. Callers frequently reference KBB values when discussing trade-ins.

### Real Estate

- **MLS (Multiple Listing Service):** The database where real estate agents list properties for sale. MLS number is the unique identifier for each listing.
- **Listing price / Asking price:** The price the seller is advertising. This may differ from the final sale price after negotiation.
- **Appraisal:** An independent assessment of a property's market value, typically required by lenders before approving a mortgage.
- **Escrow:** A neutral third-party account that holds funds and documents during a real estate transaction until all conditions are met.
- **Contingency:** A condition that must be met before a sale can close (e.g., financing contingency, inspection contingency, appraisal contingency).
- **Pre-approval:** A lender's written commitment to lend up to a certain amount, based on the buyer's financial review. Stronger than pre-qualification.
- **CMA (Comparative Market Analysis):** A report comparing a property to similar recently sold properties to estimate market value.

### Solar

- **kWh (kilowatt-hour):** The standard unit of electricity consumption. Used to measure how much energy a solar system produces and how much the home uses.
- **Net metering:** A billing arrangement where excess solar energy sent back to the grid earns credits on the homeowner's utility bill.
- **ITC (Investment Tax Credit):** A federal tax credit (currently 30%) that applies to the total cost of a solar installation, including equipment and labor.
- **Panel efficiency:** The percentage of sunlight a solar panel converts into usable electricity. Higher efficiency panels produce more power per square foot.
- **Payback period:** The time it takes for energy savings to equal the cost of the solar installation. Typically 5-10 years depending on location and utility rates.
- **PPA (Power Purchase Agreement):** An arrangement where a third party owns and maintains the solar system, and the homeowner buys the electricity at a fixed rate.

### Insurance

- **Premium:** The amount paid (monthly, quarterly, or annually) to maintain insurance coverage.
- **Deductible:** The amount the policyholder pays out of pocket before insurance coverage kicks in. Higher deductible usually means lower premium.
- **Coverage limit:** The maximum amount an insurer will pay for a covered claim.
- **Liability coverage:** Pays for damages or injuries the policyholder causes to others.
- **Comprehensive coverage:** Covers non-collision damage (theft, weather, vandalism, animal strikes).
- **Collision coverage:** Covers damage to the policyholder's vehicle from a collision, regardless of fault.
- **Umbrella policy:** Additional liability coverage that extends beyond the limits of existing auto, home, or other policies.

## 2. Common Financing Terms

- **APR (Annual Percentage Rate):** The total yearly cost of borrowing, including interest and fees, expressed as a percentage.
- **Term length:** The duration of the loan (e.g., 36 months, 60 months, 72 months). Longer terms mean lower monthly payments but more total interest.
- **Down payment:** The upfront cash amount paid at the time of purchase, reducing the total financed amount.
- **Monthly payment:** The recurring amount due each month, determined by the loan amount, APR, and term length.
- **Pre-approval:** A lender's conditional commitment to a loan amount, based on a preliminary review of the buyer's credit and finances.
- **Credit check (hard pull):** A lender's inquiry into the buyer's credit history. Occurs during formal application and can temporarily affect credit score.
- **Co-signer:** A second person who agrees to be responsible for the loan if the primary borrower cannot pay. Used when the primary borrower's credit is insufficient.

## 3. Trade-In Process Steps

1. **Vehicle inspection:** The dealership examines the vehicle's exterior, interior, mechanical condition, and mileage.
2. **Market value assessment:** The dealer references current market data (auction values, KBB, comparable sales) to determine a fair offer.
3. **Offer:** The dealership presents a trade-in value to the owner, which is applied as a credit toward the new purchase.
4. **Negotiation:** The owner can accept, counter, or decline the offer. Having outside appraisals (CarMax, KBB Instant Cash Offer) strengthens their position.
5. **Title transfer:** Once agreed, the owner signs over the vehicle title to the dealership. Any remaining loan balance must be paid off at this stage.

## 4. General Sales Process Flow

Every sales call follows this fundamental progression, regardless of industry:

1. **Greeting:** Warm, professional introduction. State your name and the business. Ask how you can help.
2. **Qualifying questions:** Determine what the caller needs, their budget, timeline, and decision-making situation. Listen more than you talk.
3. **Needs assessment:** Based on their answers, identify the right product, service, or solution category. Confirm your understanding back to them.
4. **Product presentation:** Briefly describe what matches their needs. Provide enough detail to build interest, but save the full experience for the in-person appointment.
5. **Objection handling:** Address concerns calmly and confidently. Empathize, answer directly, and redirect. Refer to the Objection Handling Playbook for specific scripts.
6. **Closing (appointment booking):** Propose a specific day and time. Confirm the appointment details, location, and what to bring or expect.
7. **Follow-up:** Confirm the appointment via text or email. Send a reminder before the scheduled time. If they didn't book, follow up within 24 hours with a summary and invitation to reschedule.

This flow is a guide, not a rigid script. Adapt to the caller's pace, energy, and needs. Some callers will move quickly to booking. Others need more time in the qualifying and objection phases. Meet them where they are.

## RV / Marine Terminology

- **Class A:** Largest motorhome, built on a bus or commercial truck chassis. 26-45 feet.
- **Class B:** Camper van. Compact, built on a standard van chassis. 16-22 feet.
- **Class C:** Mid-size motorhome with overcab sleeping area. 20-33 feet. Built on truck/van chassis.
- **Fifth Wheel:** Large towable RV that hitches in the truck bed. Requires a fifth-wheel hitch.
- **Travel Trailer:** Towable RV that hitches to a standard ball hitch. Various sizes.
- **Toy Hauler:** RV with a built-in garage for ATVs, motorcycles, or gear.
- **Slide-Out:** Section of the RV that extends outward when parked to create more interior space.
- **Dry Weight / GVWR:** Dry weight is the RV without fluids or cargo. GVWR is the max loaded weight.
- **Towing Capacity:** Maximum weight a tow vehicle can safely pull. Critical for towable RVs.
- **Beam:** Width of a boat at its widest point.
- **Draft:** How deep the boat sits in the water. Important for shallow lakes/rivers.
- **LOA (Length Overall):** Total length of a boat from bow to stern.
- **Horsepower (HP):** Engine power rating for boats and powersports.
- **Pontoon:** Flat-bottomed boat supported by pontoons (tubes). Popular for lake recreation.
- **Center Console:** Fishing boat with the steering console in the center. Open deck design.
- **Bow Rider:** Recreational boat with seating in the bow area.
- **Stern Drive / Inboard-Outboard (I/O):** Engine inside the boat with drive unit on the transom.

## Technology / SaaS Sales Terminology

- **SaaS (Software as a Service):** Software delivered via the internet on a subscription basis.
- **ARR / MRR:** Annual Recurring Revenue / Monthly Recurring Revenue.
- **Seat / License:** Per-user pricing unit. "How many seats do you need?"
- **Implementation:** The process of setting up, configuring, and deploying software for a customer.
- **Onboarding:** Training users to adopt and use the software effectively.
- **SLA (Service Level Agreement):** Guaranteed uptime and response time commitments.
- **API Integration:** Connecting the software with other systems the customer uses.
- **SSO (Single Sign-On):** Allowing users to log in with their existing company credentials.
- **Data Migration:** Moving data from old systems to the new platform.
- **POC / Proof of Concept:** A limited trial to demonstrate the software works for the customer's use case.
- **TCO (Total Cost of Ownership):** Full cost including license, implementation, training, and maintenance.
- **ROI (Return on Investment):** The financial benefit relative to cost. Key decision-making metric.
- **Churn:** When a customer cancels their subscription.
- **Upsell / Cross-sell:** Selling additional features (upsell) or related products (cross-sell) to existing customers.
- **Enterprise Agreement:** Custom pricing and terms for large organizations.

## Equipment Sales Terminology

- **Lease vs Buy:** Leasing = lower monthly payments, return at end of term. Buying = ownership, asset on balance sheet.
- **Section 179:** IRS tax code allowing businesses to deduct the full purchase price of qualifying equipment in the year of purchase.
- **Bonus Depreciation:** Additional first-year depreciation deduction.
- **Residual Value:** Estimated value of equipment at the end of a lease term.
- **TCO (Total Cost of Ownership):** Purchase price plus maintenance, fuel, insurance, and downtime costs over the equipment's life.
- **ROI (Return on Investment):** How quickly the equipment pays for itself through increased productivity or revenue.
- **Preventive Maintenance (PM):** Scheduled maintenance to prevent breakdowns and extend equipment life.
- **Utilization Rate:** Percentage of time equipment is actively being used. Key metric for fleet decisions.
- **Lead Time:** Time from order to delivery. Can be weeks to months for specialized equipment.
- **Attachment:** Accessory that adds functionality (e.g., bucket, blade, fork for excavators).
- **Hour Meter:** Tracks total operating hours. Equipment equivalent of an odometer.
- **Undercarriage:** The bottom components of tracked equipment (bulldozers, excavators). Major maintenance cost.

## Art Gallery Terminology

- **Provenance:** The documented history of ownership of an artwork. Critical for authenticity and value.
- **Medium:** The material used to create the art — oil on canvas, acrylic, watercolor, bronze, mixed media.
- **Edition:** For prints and sculpture, the total number produced. "Edition of 25" means 25 were made.
- **Artist Proof (AP):** Prints outside the main edition, reserved for the artist. Typically numbered separately.
- **Giclee:** High-quality digital print on canvas or fine art paper.
- **Original:** One-of-a-kind artwork created by the artist's hand.
- **Commission:** A custom piece created to the buyer's specifications.
- **Framing:** Gallery framing includes archival materials and UV-protective glass.
- **Certificate of Authenticity (COA):** Document certifying the artwork is genuine.
- **Art Advisory:** Consulting service to help collectors build or curate their collection.
- **Primary Market:** Art sold for the first time (directly from artist or gallery).
- **Secondary Market:** Resale of previously owned artwork (auction houses, private sales).
