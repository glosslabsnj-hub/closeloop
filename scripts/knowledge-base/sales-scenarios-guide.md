# Sales Scenarios Guide

Reference document for the CloseLoop Sales Agent. This guide covers realistic caller scenarios across 9 industry verticals. For each scenario, use the caller context, qualifying questions, and recommended approach to guide the conversation toward booking an appointment.

---

## 1. Car Dealership Scenarios

### Scenario 1A: New Buyer (First-Time Purchase)

**Caller situation:** A first-time car buyer, often younger (early 20s), who is uncertain about the buying process. They may have been browsing online and have a vague idea of what they want but lack confidence in their budget, financing options, and which vehicle fits their needs.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- What will you primarily use the vehicle for -- commuting, family, recreation?
- Do you have a budget range in mind, including monthly payment targets?
- Have you been pre-approved for financing, or would you like us to help with that?
- Is there a specific make or model you've been researching?
- When are you looking to make a purchase -- this week, this month?

**Recommended approach:** Be patient and educational. Avoid jargon. Reassure them that the process is straightforward and that visiting the dealership is a no-pressure experience. Emphasize that a test drive helps them feel confident in their decision. Position the appointment as a "guided tour" rather than a sales pitch.

### Scenario 1B: Returning Customer (Upgrade/Repeat Buy)

**Caller situation:** A previous customer who bought or leased from this dealership before. They may be approaching lease-end or want to upgrade. They already trust the dealership to some degree.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- When did you last purchase or lease with us, and what vehicle do you currently have?
- Is your lease coming up for renewal, or are you looking to buy this time?
- What's prompting the change -- growing family, lifestyle shift, mileage needs?
- Are you interested in the latest model year of what you have, or something different?

**Recommended approach:** Acknowledge their loyalty. Reference the fact that returning customers often qualify for loyalty incentives. Make the process feel easy -- "Since you've been through this before, we can make it quick." Push toward an appointment with their previous salesperson if possible.

### Scenario 1C: Trade-In Inquiry

**Caller situation:** The caller wants to know what their current vehicle is worth before committing to a new purchase. They may have checked KBB or Edmunds and want to validate that number.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- What year, make, model, and trim is your current vehicle?
- Approximately how many miles are on it?
- What's the overall condition -- any accidents, mechanical issues, or cosmetic damage?
- Are you looking to trade toward a specific vehicle, or just exploring your options?

**Recommended approach:** Validate their research. Explain that online estimates are a starting point, but an in-person appraisal gives the most accurate and often higher value. Position the appointment as a "free, no-obligation appraisal" that takes about 15 minutes.

### Scenario 1D: Financing Questions

**Caller situation:** The caller is focused on monthly payments and whether they can qualify. They may have credit concerns or want to compare dealer financing against their bank or credit union.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- Do you have a target monthly payment you're comfortable with?
- Have you checked with your bank or credit union, or would you like us to shop rates for you?
- Do you have a down payment planned?
- Are you aware of your approximate credit score range?

**Recommended approach:** Be reassuring, not judgmental. Emphasize that the dealership works with multiple lenders and can often find competitive rates. Avoid quoting specific rates over the phone. The goal is to get them in for a conversation with the finance team. Frame it as "Let's see what we can put together for you."

### Scenario 1E: Comparison Shopping

**Caller situation:** The caller is actively comparing this dealership against competitors. They may mention a specific price or offer from another dealer. They are looking for a reason to choose this dealership.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- Which vehicles or dealerships are you comparing?
- Is price the main factor, or are you also weighing things like service, warranty, or location?
- Do you have a written offer from another dealer we can look at?
- What would make you feel confident choosing us?

**Recommended approach:** Never badmouth competitors. Instead, highlight differentiators: service department reputation, warranty packages, customer experience, inventory availability. Offer to match or beat documented offers where possible, but always in person. The appointment is positioned as "Let us earn your business -- come see the difference."

## 2. Real Estate Scenarios

### Scenario 2A: Buyer Inquiry (Active Home Search)

**Caller situation:** A buyer who saw a listing online (Zillow, Realtor.com, MLS) and wants more details. They may or may not have an agent yet.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- Which property caught your eye, and what specifically interested you about it?
- Are you currently working with a real estate agent?
- Have you been pre-approved for a mortgage? What's your budget range?
- What's your timeline -- are you looking to move within the next 30, 60, 90 days?
- Are there must-haves like school district, number of bedrooms, or lot size?

**Recommended approach:** Provide enough detail to keep them engaged, but make it clear that seeing the property in person is essential. Offer to schedule a private showing. If they don't have an agent, position your agent as a resource who can help them navigate the full process.

### Scenario 2B: Seller Listing Inquiry

**Caller situation:** A homeowner thinking about selling. They want to know what their home is worth and what the process looks like.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- What's prompting you to consider selling -- relocating, upgrading, downsizing?
- How long have you owned the property?
- Have you done any major renovations or updates recently?
- Do you have a timeline in mind for when you'd like to list?

**Recommended approach:** Offer a free comparative market analysis (CMA). Explain that an in-person walkthrough gives a much more accurate valuation than online estimates. Position the appointment as a consultation, not a commitment to list.

### Scenario 2C: Investment Property

**Caller situation:** An investor looking at properties for rental income, fix-and-flip, or portfolio growth. They are numbers-driven and want ROI data.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- Are you looking for rental income, appreciation, or a flip opportunity?
- What's your investment budget and financing approach -- cash, conventional, portfolio loan?
- Do you have a target cap rate or cash-on-cash return?
- Are you focused on a specific neighborhood or property type?

**Recommended approach:** Speak their language -- mention cap rates, rental comps, and market trends. Offer to set up a meeting to review a curated list of properties that match their investment criteria. Position the agent as a market expert who can identify off-market deals.

### Scenario 2D: Relocation

**Caller situation:** Someone moving to the area from out of state or out of town. They may have limited knowledge of neighborhoods and need guidance.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- Where are you relocating from, and what's bringing you to the area?
- When do you need to be settled -- is there a job start date or school enrollment deadline?
- What matters most to you in a neighborhood -- commute, schools, walkability, price?
- Will you be visiting in person, or do you need virtual tour options initially?

**Recommended approach:** Be a local resource, not just a salesperson. Offer neighborhood overviews and virtual tours for remote buyers. The appointment can be positioned as a "relocation consultation" to map out their search strategy.

## 3. Solar / Home Improvement Scenarios

### Scenario 3A: Homeowner Initial Inquiry

**Caller situation:** A homeowner who has seen ads or heard from a neighbor about solar and is curious but not deeply informed.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- What's your average monthly electricity bill?
- Do you own your home, and roughly how old is your roof?
- What's your primary motivation -- saving money, environmental, energy independence?
- Have you looked into solar before, or is this your first exploration?

**Recommended approach:** Educate without overwhelming. Use simple comparisons: "Most homeowners in your area save 30-50% on their electric bill." Offer a free home energy assessment. Emphasize that the consultation is no-obligation and takes about 45 minutes.

### Scenario 3B: Cost and Financing Questions

**Caller situation:** The caller has done some research and is focused on cost -- system price, monthly payments, and whether it's actually worth it financially.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- Have you received any quotes already?
- Are you more interested in purchasing outright, financing, or a lease/PPA arrangement?
- Are you aware of the federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC) and any state or local incentives?
- What does your current utility rate look like, and has it been increasing?

**Recommended approach:** Avoid quoting exact prices over the phone since every home is different. Explain that a site assessment determines system size and exact savings. Mention the ITC (currently 30% federal tax credit) and any state incentives. The appointment is framed as a "custom savings analysis."

### Scenario 3C: Timeline and Installation Process

**Caller situation:** The caller is close to deciding but wants to understand logistics -- permitting, installation time, when they start saving.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- Is there a specific deadline you're working toward, like a utility rate change or a tax year?
- Have you already had a roof inspection or know its condition?
- Are there any HOA restrictions we should be aware of?

**Recommended approach:** Walk through the typical timeline: consultation, design, permitting, installation, inspection, activation. Set realistic expectations (usually 6-12 weeks total). The appointment kicks off the process and locks in current incentive rates.

### Scenario 3D: Tax Incentive Questions

**Caller situation:** The caller is primarily motivated by tax benefits and wants specifics on credits and deductions.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- Do you typically owe federal taxes, or do you receive a refund? (Affects ITC usability.)
- Are you aware that the ITC applies to the full system cost including installation?
- Have you spoken with your tax advisor about solar benefits?

**Recommended approach:** Provide general information about the ITC and state incentives, but advise them to consult their tax professional for personal advice. Emphasize that during the in-home consultation, the proposal will include a full financial breakdown including estimated tax benefits.

## 4. Insurance Scenarios

### Scenario 4A: Switching Providers

**Caller situation:** The caller's current insurance is too expensive, or they had a bad claims experience. They want to see if they can get a better deal.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- What type of coverage are you looking to switch -- auto, home, life, or a bundle?
- Who is your current provider, and when does your policy renew?
- Have you had any claims in the last 3-5 years?
- What's your current premium, and what would you like to see it at?

**Recommended approach:** Empathize with their frustration. Explain that a quick review of their current policy can identify gaps or overcharges. Position the appointment as a "policy review" -- free and no-obligation, typically 20-30 minutes.

### Scenario 4B: New Policy (First-Time Buyer)

**Caller situation:** A first-time home buyer, new driver, or someone who has never had their own policy.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- What triggered the need for coverage -- new home, new car, life event?
- What coverage types are you looking for?
- Do you have a budget in mind for monthly premiums?

**Recommended approach:** Simplify the process. Explain what each coverage type does in plain language. Avoid overwhelming them with options on the phone. Book a consultation to build a personalized coverage plan.

### Scenario 4C: Adding Coverage (Life Event)

**Caller situation:** The caller recently got married, had a baby, bought a home, or started a business and needs to update or expand their coverage.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- What life event is prompting the change?
- Do you currently have any coverage, and with whom?
- Are you looking to add to your existing policy or start fresh?

**Recommended approach:** Congratulate them on the life event. Explain that bundling coverage often saves money. Offer to review their full picture in an appointment to make sure they're fully protected without overpaying.

### Scenario 4D: Price Shopping

**Caller situation:** The caller is collecting quotes from multiple providers and will go with the cheapest option.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- What coverage types and limits are you comparing?
- Are you comparing apples to apples -- same deductibles, same coverage limits?
- Is price the only factor, or do claims service and agent accessibility matter?

**Recommended approach:** Avoid a race to the bottom on price alone. Emphasize value: claims support, local agent access, coverage quality. Offer a side-by-side comparison appointment where they can bring competitor quotes.

## 5. Equipment / Machinery Scenarios

### Scenario 5A: Commercial Buyer (Known Need)

**Caller situation:** A business owner or procurement manager who knows exactly what equipment they need and wants pricing and availability.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- What specific equipment or model are you looking for?
- What's the application -- construction, agriculture, manufacturing, landscaping?
- What's your timeline for delivery?
- Are you buying for a single unit or a fleet?

**Recommended approach:** Be efficient and knowledgeable. Confirm availability and provide a general price range. For exact quotes, schedule a meeting with a sales specialist who can configure the right package including attachments, warranties, and delivery.

### Scenario 5B: Lease vs. Buy Decision

**Caller situation:** The caller is weighing whether to purchase outright, finance, or lease equipment. They want to understand the financial implications.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- How long do you anticipate needing this equipment -- seasonal, project-based, or permanent?
- What's your preference on maintenance responsibility?
- Have you explored Section 179 tax deductions for purchased equipment?
- What does your cash flow situation look like -- do you prefer lower monthly costs or ownership?

**Recommended approach:** Outline the pros and cons of each option briefly. Explain that a sit-down with the finance team can model different scenarios with exact numbers. Book the appointment as a "financial options review."

### Scenario 5C: Demo Request

**Caller situation:** The caller wants to see equipment in action before committing. They may be comparing brands or models.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- What equipment are you interested in demoing?
- Do you have a specific jobsite or application in mind for the demo?
- Who will be operating the equipment -- do they need to be present for the demo?
- When works best for your schedule?

**Recommended approach:** Demos are a powerful closing tool. Make scheduling easy and flexible. Offer on-site demos if available. Emphasize that there's no obligation -- it's about making sure the equipment fits their needs.

## 6. Luxury Retail Scenarios

### Scenario 6A: Special Occasion Purchase

**Caller situation:** The caller is shopping for a milestone -- anniversary, birthday, graduation, promotion. They want something memorable but may not know exactly what.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- What's the occasion, and who is it for?
- Do you have a particular style or category in mind -- jewelry, watches, accessories?
- What's your budget range?
- When is the occasion? Do we have time for custom or special order?

**Recommended approach:** Make them feel special. Luxury is about experience, not transactions. Offer a private appointment with a personal stylist or consultant. Emphasize the exclusivity and personalization of an in-store visit.

### Scenario 6B: Gift Buying (Unsure What to Get)

**Caller situation:** The caller knows they want to buy a gift but has no idea what to choose. They need guidance.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- Tell me about the person you're shopping for -- their style, age, interests?
- Have they mentioned anything they've been wanting?
- What's your budget range?
- Would you prefer something classic and timeless or trendy and modern?

**Recommended approach:** Position yourself as a helpful guide. Suggest a curated selection viewing by appointment. Mention gift wrapping, engraving, or personalization services. Make the experience feel effortless for the buyer.

### Scenario 6C: Custom Order

**Caller situation:** The caller wants something made to order -- monogrammed, engraved, bespoke sizing, or a one-of-a-kind piece.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- What type of item are you looking to customize?
- What personalization do you have in mind?
- What's your timeline -- is this for a specific date?
- Have you worked with us on custom pieces before?

**Recommended approach:** Emphasize craftsmanship and exclusivity. Explain that custom orders require an in-person consultation to review options, materials, and design. Book the appointment as a "design consultation."

## 7. Furniture / Appliance Scenarios

### Scenario 7A: Browsing / Early Stage

**Caller situation:** The caller is furnishing a new home or replacing items but hasn't narrowed down choices yet.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- What rooms or areas are you furnishing?
- Do you have a style preference -- modern, traditional, transitional?
- What's your overall budget for this project?
- Have you measured your spaces?

**Recommended approach:** Invite them to visit the showroom. Mention that an in-person visit lets them see materials, test comfort, and get design advice. Offer to pair them with a design consultant at no extra charge.

### Scenario 7B: Sizing and Fit Questions

**Caller situation:** The caller has a specific space and needs to know if a piece will fit. They may have measurements.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- What are the dimensions of your space, including doorways and hallways for delivery?
- Are there any obstacles -- stairs, tight corners, elevators?
- Have you identified a specific piece, or are you open to alternatives that fit better?

**Recommended approach:** Offer to check dimensions against their space. For complex situations, suggest a showroom visit where a consultant can help with space planning. Mention delivery assessment services if available.

### Scenario 7C: Delivery and Timeline

**Caller situation:** The caller needs items by a specific date -- moving day, event, renovation completion.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- What's your target delivery date?
- Is the item in stock, or would it need to be ordered?
- What's your delivery address, and are there any access challenges?

**Recommended approach:** Be transparent about lead times. If the timeline is tight, explore in-stock options or expedited delivery. Book a showroom appointment to finalize selections quickly.

### Scenario 7D: Financing Questions

**Caller situation:** The caller is interested in financing a large furniture or appliance purchase.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- What's the approximate total of what you're looking to purchase?
- Are you interested in 0% promotional financing, or standard monthly payments?
- Have you financed with us before?

**Recommended approach:** Mention available financing promotions (e.g., "0% for 12 months on purchases over $999"). Explain that financing approval is quick and can be done during a showroom visit. Position the appointment as a chance to select items and get approved in one visit.

## 8. Jewelry Scenarios

### Scenario 8A: Engagement Ring Shopping

**Caller situation:** The caller is planning a proposal and is either overwhelmed by options or has done extensive research. This is a high-emotion, high-stakes purchase.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- Do you have a style in mind -- solitaire, halo, vintage, modern?
- What's your budget range for the ring?
- Do you know your partner's ring size?
- What's your timeline for the proposal?
- Does your partner have a preference on diamond shape or gemstone?

**Recommended approach:** Be warm and congratulatory. Acknowledge how important this decision is. Reassure them that a private consultation will make the process enjoyable, not stressful. Offer to show a curated selection based on their preferences. Never rush this buyer.

### Scenario 8B: Anniversary or Milestone Gift

**Caller situation:** The caller is buying for a spouse or partner for an anniversary, birthday, or milestone. They want something meaningful.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- What anniversary or milestone are you celebrating?
- What does your partner typically wear -- gold, silver, platinum? Minimalist or statement pieces?
- Do they have a wishlist or have they dropped hints?
- What's your budget range?

**Recommended approach:** Suggest pieces that align with the milestone (e.g., eternity band for anniversary, birthstone for birthday). Offer engraving or personalization. Book a private viewing appointment.

### Scenario 8C: Custom Design

**Caller situation:** The caller wants a unique piece designed from scratch or wants to repurpose existing stones or metals.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- Do you have existing pieces or stones you'd like to incorporate?
- Do you have design inspiration -- sketches, photos, references?
- What's your budget and timeline?
- Is this for yourself or a gift?

**Recommended approach:** Emphasize the artistry and personal meaning of custom jewelry. Explain the design process: consultation, sketch/CAD, approval, creation. Book a design consultation appointment. Mention typical timelines (4-8 weeks for custom work).

### Scenario 8D: Appraisal or Repair

**Caller situation:** The caller has an existing piece that needs appraisal (for insurance) or repair (broken clasp, missing stone, resizing).

**Key qualifying questions:**
- What type of service do you need -- appraisal, repair, or resizing?
- Can you describe the piece and the issue?
- Do you need this for insurance documentation?
- Is there a timeline -- upcoming event or insurance renewal?

**Recommended approach:** Be helpful and service-oriented. Explain that an in-person evaluation is needed to provide an accurate quote and timeline. Book a service appointment. Mention that while they're in, they can browse new arrivals.

## 9. Home Builder Scenarios

### Scenario 9A: Model Home Tour

**Caller situation:** The caller saw a sign, ad, or online listing for a new community and wants to see model homes. They may be early in their search.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- Which community or floor plan caught your interest?
- Are you looking to build new, or also considering existing homes?
- What's your timeline for moving?
- How many bedrooms and bathrooms do you need?
- Do you currently own a home that you'd need to sell?

**Recommended approach:** Build excitement about the community and the model homes. Mention any current incentives or limited-time pricing. Book a private tour so they get undivided attention from a sales consultant, rather than a walk-in experience.

### Scenario 9B: Lot Selection

**Caller situation:** The caller is serious about building and wants to understand lot availability, pricing, and differences between lots (size, view, premium, HOA).

**Key qualifying questions:**
- Do you have a preferred lot size or location within the community -- corner, cul-de-sac, view lot?
- Are you aware of lot premiums and what affects pricing?
- Have you already selected a floor plan, or does the lot choice come first for you?
- What's your overall budget for lot plus home?

**Recommended approach:** Explain that lot availability changes quickly and the best way to secure a preferred lot is to visit and review the site map in person. Offer to hold a lot for a limited time if that's a possibility. Book a site visit appointment.

### Scenario 9C: Customization and Design Center

**Caller situation:** The caller wants to know how much they can customize -- upgrades, finishes, structural changes. They want to make the home theirs.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- What aspects of customization are most important to you -- kitchen, flooring, exterior, structural layout?
- Have you visited our design center or seen our standard versus upgraded options?
- What's your budget for upgrades beyond the base price?
- Are there any must-haves that would be dealbreakers if not available?

**Recommended approach:** Emphasize the range of customization available. Mention the design center experience where they can see and touch materials. Explain that a sales consultant can walk them through what's included and what's upgradeable. Book a combined model tour and design center appointment.

### Scenario 9D: Timeline and Construction Process

**Caller situation:** The caller needs to coordinate building with selling their current home, a lease expiration, or a job relocation date.

**Key qualifying questions:**
- What's your ideal move-in date?
- Do you have a current home to sell, or are you renting?
- Are you familiar with the typical construction timeline in this area?
- Would you be interested in a move-in-ready spec home if one matches your needs?

**Recommended approach:** Be transparent about construction timelines (typically 6-12 months depending on the market and customization). If timing is tight, suggest spec homes or quick-move-in options. Book an appointment to review available inventory and timeline options with a sales consultant.

## 10. RV / Boat / Powersports Scenarios

### Scenario 10A: First-Time RV Buyer
**Caller situation:** Family considering their first RV for road trips. Overwhelmed by Class A vs B vs C, towable vs motorized.
**Key qualifying questions:**
- How will you primarily use it — weekend getaways, extended travel, full-time living?
- How many people typically travel with you?
- Do you have a tow vehicle, and do you know its towing capacity?
- What's your budget range — are you looking new or pre-owned?
- Do you have a storage plan?
**Recommended approach:** Be educational. Explain the differences between RV classes simply. Suggest a walkthrough appointment to sit in different layouts. Mention that test drives help them understand drivability.

### Scenario 10B: Boat Buyer (Fishing/Recreation)
**Caller situation:** Experienced or first-time boater looking for a fishing boat, pontoon, ski boat, or cruiser.
**Key qualifying questions:**
- What type of boating — fishing, skiing, cruising, entertaining?
- What body of water — lake, river, coastal, offshore?
- How many passengers typically?
- Trailer or slip/dock storage?
- New or pre-owned preference?
**Recommended approach:** Match the boat type to their use case. Invite them for a dock-side viewing or sea trial. Mention any boat shows or demo days coming up.

### Scenario 10C: Motorcycle/Powersports Enthusiast
**Caller situation:** Rider looking for a motorcycle, ATV, UTV, or side-by-side. May be upgrading or buying their first.
**Key qualifying questions:**
- What type of riding — street, off-road, touring, sport?
- What's your experience level?
- Any specific make or model you're looking at?
- Do you need financing or gear/accessories?
**Recommended approach:** Match them with the right category. Offer a test ride for motorcycles. For ATVs/UTVs, offer a demo on your test track if available.

- What's your timeline?
**Recommended approach:** Explain the upfit process and timeline. Offer to coordinate with upfit vendors. Schedule a consultation to spec out the build.

## General Principles Across All Scenarios

1. **Always qualify before pitching.** Ask questions first. Understand the caller's situation before recommending anything.
2. **Never pressure.** The goal is to be helpful, build trust, and earn the appointment.
3. **Acknowledge emotions.** Whether it's excitement (engagement ring, new home) or frustration (insurance, price shopping), meet the caller where they are.
4. **Redirect to in-person.** The phone call is not where the sale closes. The purpose is to book the appointment where a human expert can deliver the full experience.
5. **Capture contact information.** Before ending any call, confirm the caller's name, phone number, and email for follow-up.
6. **Set clear next steps.** Every call should end with a confirmed appointment time or a specific follow-up action.
